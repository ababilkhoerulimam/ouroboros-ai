# F4: Which Entities and When

Question: **which entities repeat, and where do they stop?**

F2 measured how much differs. F3 split that difference into components. F4 answers a
different question: is the pattern coming from the same entities behaving
differently, or from a different mix of entities arriving and leaving? Those two
produce different actions, and only one of them is visible in a total.

F4 has two instruments, and both are optional. A cohort needs an entity that
repeats. A funnel needs an ordered stage. If the data has neither, F4 does not run,
and saying so is the correct output.

## Entry condition

F1's column-role table decides whether F4 has work. Check two roles:

```
entity key          = usable   # a value that repeats across rows or periods
category with order = usable   # a status or stage column whose values progress
```

| Verdict | F4 does |
|---|---|
| entity key usable | cohort of first appearance |
| stage column usable | stage funnel and drop-off |
| both | cohort first, funnel second, one chart total |
| neither | skip the phase, see below |

## When F4 does not apply

Do not invent an entity. A row identifier is not an entity: every row is unique by
construction, so treating it as one yields a cohort that never returns, and a
retention chart that says 0 percent forever.

Before skipping, print the evidence for the skip:

```
id_candidates=row_id,device_id  repeat_rate_row_id=0.0000  repeat_rate_device_id=0.4180
```

A key is an entity key only when it repeats across periods. Print the share of rows
whose key appears in more than one period. If no candidate key repeats, there is no
cohort to measure.

Then close the phase properly:

1. Write `cannot_answer`: "retensi entitas: tidak ada identifier yang berulang" and
   "funnel: tidak ada kolom tahapan".
2. Write `evidence/F4.json` with the skip finding: which columns were tested and
   their repeat rates. A skip is a measurement of the data, and it carries an
   `E-###` like any other.
3. `advance_phase("F5", "F4 tidak berlaku: <reason>")`. The controller records it in
   `done.json` under `phases_skipped`.

Do not draw an empty chart to fill the section. Do not write a paragraph explaining
that retention would have been interesting.

## Is the key usable

A repeat rate above zero is necessary, not sufficient.

- Normalise before counting: trim whitespace, lower-case identifiers, strip phone
  prefixes and separators, collapse obvious typos. Print distinct counts before and
  after normalisation; a merge that halves the distinct count is a finding, not a
  cleanup detail.
- Print coverage: the share of rows with a non-null key. Anonymous rows are usually
  null, and those entities are invisible, not gone.
- Coverage below 70 percent makes the cohort a statement about identified entities
  only. Say that in the evidence statement and set `confidence: "medium"`.
- One entity with two identifiers (a serial number on one row, a name on another) is
  two entities to a naive groupby. If both columns exist, print how often they
  disagree on the same row before trusting either.

## Step order

| # | Step | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | key test: repeat rate per candidate, coverage, normalisation effect | `key=… repeat=… coverage=…` |
| 2 | cohort definition: first appearance period per entity | cohort sizes per period |
| 3 | cohort matrix: cohort × periods since first appearance | counts and rates, future cells masked |
| 4 | fixed-horizon rate: M1, M3, M6 across cohorts | one rate per horizon, with cohort count |
| 5 | new versus returning split of the primary measure | share of the measure and of the row count |
| 6 | stage order test, then funnel counts | counts per stage, step and cumulative rates |
| 7 | drop-off per step, with the worst step named | count lost, rate, share of total loss |
| 8 | segment contrast, one dimension only | rate by segment, with n |

## Cohorts

A cohort is the set of entities whose first appearance falls in one period.
Retention at M*k* is the share of that cohort present in the k-th period after first
appearance.

```
retention_M1 = entities_active_in_M1 / cohort_size
```

Rules that change the answer:

- **Cohort size is the denominator, always printed.** A rate without its cohort size
  cannot be judged: 50 percent of 4 entities is noise.
- **Survivorship: keep everyone in the denominator.** Never filter the cohort to
  entities still active at the end of the window, to those that never failed, or to
  those that answered a survey. A cohort defined after the outcome measures the
  survivors, not the cohort. If the export only supports the filtered view, say so,
  set `confidence: "medium"`, and name the excluded group.
- **Mask the future, never zero it.** A cohort acquired last month has no M3. Leave
  the cell empty and label the axis "belum teramati". A zero and an unobserved
  period look identical in a heatmap and mean opposite things.
- **Compare cohorts at a common horizon.** Averaging every available cell across
  cohorts of different ages weights young cohorts toward M1 and makes retention
  appear to improve. Report M1, M3, and M6 separately, each over the cohorts old
  enough to have that horizon.
- **Censoring at the window edge.** If the export ends mid-period, the final period
  is partial for everyone. Either drop it or normalise to a daily rate, exactly as
  F2 does, and say which.
- **Retention is not repeat rate.** Repeat rate is the share of entities with two or
  more appearances in the whole window; retention is presence in a specific period.
  The two numbers differ and must not be quoted interchangeably.
- **Short windows.** With fewer than three complete periods after first appearance,
  M1 is the only horizon that exists. Report M1 and write the rest into
  `cannot_answer` rather than extrapolating.

## Funnel

A funnel needs an order, not just stages. Print the distinct stage values and their
counts before anything else; a typo or a synonym in that column splits a stage in two
and lowers every downstream rate.

Establish the order from the data. If the column is a category with no progression
(type, region), there is no funnel, and that goes in `cannot_answer`. If the order is
an assumption, state it once and mark every rate built on it as an assumption, not a
measurement.

Terminal states are exits, not steps. Cancelled, failed, and expired rows leave the
funnel before step one; count them separately and report them as their own line. A
funnel that ends in "cancelled" measures nothing.

State which counting model you used, because they are not interchangeable:

| Model | Data shape | Counts | What it can answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| snapshot | one row per entity, current status | exclusive: each entity sits at its furthest stage | where entities are now |
| event log | one row per transition | cumulative: reached-set per stage | where entities stopped |

A snapshot cannot show earlier progress that was overwritten by a later status, and
an event log cannot be read as if each stage held only its own count. Mixing the two
double counts.

Print both rates for every step:

```
step_rate_i = reached_i / reached_{i-1}
cum_rate_i  = reached_i / reached_1
```

Reporting only the final conversion hides the step that actually costs progress.

## Drop-off

- Report the loss per step in absolute count, not only as a percentage. A 90 percent
  loss from a tiny stage and a 20 percent loss from the biggest stage are different
  problems, and only the counts tell them apart.
- Name the worst step by absolute loss and, separately, the worst step by rate. They
  are often different steps. State both, and say which one you are acting on.
- `share_of_total_loss = lost_i / (reached_1 - reached_last)` must sum to 1.0 across
  steps. Print the sum; if it is not 1.0, a stage is counted twice or missing.
- Below 30 rows at a step, the rate is noise. Print n, set `confidence: "medium"`,
  and do not rank that step as the worst.
- Fix one time window for every entity in the funnel, counted from the first event.
  Without it, older entities had more time to progress and the funnel measures age
  instead of friction.
- Quantity at stake: convert lost rows to the primary measure only when the measure
  is additive and the per-row value is computable. Then print both inputs and the
  product. If it is not computable, report counts only and put the measure version in
  `cannot_answer`.

## Segment contrast

One dimension, never two. Two dimensions multiply the cells and most of them end up
under 30 observations.

- Cohort: M1 rate per segment, with the cohort size of each segment.
- Funnel: conversion per segment, with the reached count at each end.
- Print the largest and the smallest segment side by side. A table of ten segments is
  a data dump, not a finding.
- Direction check: if the aggregate rate is flat while every large segment improves,
  the mix shifted. This is the same Simpson test as F3, applied to rates. Record it
  as an anomaly and do not present the aggregate as the finding.

## Writing the explanation

Three sentences, in this order:

1. The number: "Dari 412 entitas, 268 muncul lagi di M1 (65,0 persen) dan 198 di M3
   (48,0 persen)."
2. The worst step: "Kehilangan terbesar ada di langkah verifikasi: 96 entitas, atau
   61 persen dari seluruh kehilangan."
3. The limit: "Data tidak mencatat alasan berhenti, jadi penyebab putus di langkah
   ini tidak dapat dipastikan."

If the phase was skipped, the report body says nothing about cohorts or funnels. The
skip becomes one bullet in "Yang tidak bisa dijawab", and the evidence file keeps the
repeat rates that justified it.

## Chart budget

At most one chart in F4.

| Situation | Chart |
|---|---|
| cohort with at least 3 cohorts and M1 to M3 observed | heatmap, percentage printed in each cell, unobserved cells blank |
| funnel, no usable cohorts | horizontal bars, counts at every step, step rate annotated |
| both available | draw the one whose finding is stronger; the other becomes a table |

The heatmap must not use a rainbow colormap, must print the percentage inside each
cell, and must leave unobserved cells empty rather than pale. The funnel bars share
one origin and print counts, because a bar of percentages cannot be checked against
the row table.

## Prohibited in F4

- Treating a row identifier as an entity.
- A cohort whose denominator is not printed.
- Zero-filling unobserved future periods.
- Averaging retention across cohorts of different maturity.
- Calling a missing identifier a loss.
- A funnel with an invented stage order, or a stage order that is not printed.
- Mixing snapshot and event-log counting in one funnel.
- Ranking a step with n below 30 as the worst step.
- Converting lost rows to the primary measure without a computable per-row value.
- Predicting retention, lifetime, or survival. That is modelling, and F5 bans it as
  well.
- More than one chart.
- A paragraph about a phase that was skipped.

## Exit check

All of these must be true before `advance_phase`:

- applicability decided and printed, with the repeat rate and coverage behind it
- if skipped: `cannot_answer` written, skip finding in `evidence/F4.json`, and the
  reason passed to `advance_phase`
- cohort size printed for every rate reported
- unobserved periods masked, not zeroed
- each horizon reported separately, over cohorts old enough to have it
- funnel counting model stated, stage order printed, terminal states pulled out
- step and cumulative rates printed with counts
- worst step named by absolute loss and by rate, with the two distinguished
- loss shares summing to 1.0
- direction check done on the largest segments, with any reversal recorded
- at most one chart, with its printed table
- `evidence/F4.json` written with one entry per claim
- `cannot_answer` updated for every horizon or stage that could not be measured
