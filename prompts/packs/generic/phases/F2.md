# F2: What Is in the Data

Question: **what does the data look like, and where do the groups differ?**

F2 measures. It does not explain. The moment you write "because", you have left
this phase.

## Entry condition

F1 is closed, and its column-role table names at least one measure and at least one
comparator: a time column or a category. Pick one primary measure now and keep it
for the whole phase.

Rule for the choice, in order:

1. the measure named in the operator brief;
2. otherwise the usable measure with the largest coefficient of variation
   (`sd / abs(mean)`), printed in the same cell;
3. ties broken by the column order in `profile.json`.

```
primary=tekanan  n=4180  cv=0.62
```

State the choice once in `notes/`, not as a finding. A metric choice is a method,
not a measurement.

## Step order

| # | Step | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | univariate profile of every usable measure | per measure: `n=… missing=… min=… p25=… median=… p75=… max=… mean=… sd=…` |
| 2 | shape and tail | mean vs median gap, skew, share in the top 10 percent, zero and negative share |
| 3 | group comparison per usable category | per group: `n=… median=… iqr=…` and the difference from the overall median |
| 4 | relationship between measures | Pearson and Spearman per pair, each with `n` |
| 5 | time axis, only if a date column exists | period grain, periods, rows per period, change against baseline |
| 6 | concentration, only if a meaningful item dimension exists | top 20 percent share, items needed for 80 percent |

Step 3 is required when the data has a usable category: without a group comparison
there is no group contrast for F3. Step 5 is required when the data has a time column.

## The headline

One of two forms. State which one you used. When the data has both a time column and
a usable category, report both and name the one F3 should explain as the headline.

**Time form.** The change between the latest complete period and its baseline,
printed with both numbers:

```
change=-0.084 value=332.0 base=362.4 base_period=2025-Q2
```

**Group form.** The difference between the largest group by row count and the rest,
or between the two largest groups. Print both medians and both counts, and name the
comparison:

```
contrast=kelompok_A_vs_B median_a=14.2 median_b=9.8 n_a=220 n_b=310
```

A difference with no base is not a finding. "Grup A lebih tinggi" is not a finding
until the other side and the group sizes are printed.

## Period selection

Only when a time column exists. Pick the coarsest grain that still gives enough
points.

| Data span | Grain | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 18 months or more | month, plus the same month last year as baseline | seasonality visible |
| 6 to 17 months | month | no year-over-year claim |
| 8 weeks to 5 months | week, with day-of-week noted | weekly noise is large |
| under 8 weeks | no trend claim at all | report totals and levels only |

Fewer than six periods is a level comparison, not a trend. Do not draw a line
through four points and call it a trend; draw bars and call it a comparison.

Print the number of periods and the row count per period before anything else. A
period with suspiciously few rows is either a partial period or a data gap, and both
change the answer.

## Partial periods

The last period is almost always incomplete. The data was exported mid-month, so the
final month looks like a collapse.

- Compute days covered per period. Print them.
- If the final period is partial, do one of two things: exclude it and say so, or
  normalise every period to a daily rate and label the chart "per hari".
- Never compare a partial period against a full one without saying it in the chart
  and in the evidence statement.

This single mistake produces more false "turun" conclusions than any other step in
the run.

## Baseline

A change with no base is not a finding.

| Situation | Baseline |
|---|---|
| 13 months or more of data | the same period last year |
| 6 to 12 months | the previous period |
| fewer than 6 periods | the average of all periods, stated as such |
| a known event (policy change, outage) | the period before the event, named |

Print the base next to the value. The statement in evidence repeats the base in
words.

## Distribution and shape

The mean is not automatically the answer.

- Print mean and median together. If they differ by more than the spread you plan
  to report, the mean is a story about a few rows.
- Print the share in the top 10 percent of rows. Above 50 percent, say that the
  measure is concentrated and that a mean describes a minority.
- Print the share of zeros and negatives. A measure that is zero most of the time
  needs a different summary than a continuous one.
- Do not trim, winsorise, or drop outliers here. F6 tests whether the conclusion
  survives trimming; F2 reports what is there.

## Group comparison

For each categorical column F1 marked usable with 2 to 12 distinct values:

- count, median, and interquartile range per group;
- the difference between each group median and the overall median;
- the group sizes, always.

Sort descending by median, not by count. A large group with a typical value is not
the interesting group.

Above 12 distinct values, keep the top 10 by count and aggregate the rest into
`lainnya`. The bucket must be labelled, and the counts must still sum to the total.
Print the residual after the bucket is added; if it is not zero, the grouping is
wrong.

Do not compare groups whose meaning changed mid-period (a renamed category, a
merged region). If F1 flagged that, skip the column and record why.

## Relationship between measures

Correlation is a measurement, not an explanation.

- Print Pearson and Spearman for each pair of usable measures, each with `n`.
- If the two disagree by more than 0.15, the tail drives the relationship. Say so
  and report the Spearman value as the headline.
- A correlation computed on fewer than 30 complete pairs is not reportable.
- A scatter plot is allowed only when both axes are continuous and the cell prints
  the correlation it illustrates.

## Concentration

Only when an item dimension exists (products, devices, accounts, regions). Two
numbers, both printed:

1. Share of the measure held by the top 20 percent of items, by count of items.
2. How many items are needed to reach 80 percent of the total.

Then one sentence in evidence: concentrated or not, with the threshold you used.
Concentration above 60 percent in the top quintile is a finding worth reporting,
because it changes what an action should target.

Do not compute Gini or HHI here. They are precise and the reader cannot act on them.

## Chart budget

At most three charts in F2.

| Chart | Worth drawing when |
|---|---|
| distribution of the primary measure (box or histogram) | always, if the shape is not obvious from the table |
| sorted bar of group medians | step 3 found a difference worth reporting |
| line of the primary measure per period | six or more periods |

Every chart follows `style.md`: title states the finding, bars from zero, direct
labels, and the same cell prints the table.

## Evidence discipline

Evidence is per claim, not per row. A table of twelve groups yields one to three
findings, not twelve.

- The headline: one `E-###`.
- The group that differs most: one `E-###`.
- The distribution summary: one `E-###`, or two when mean and median are both
  quoted.
- A group with a small count is not evidence, even if its median is extreme.

Every statement carries its base: "median kelompok A 14,2, dibanding 9,8 di
kelompok B (220 dan 310 baris)", never "kelompok A lebih tinggi".

## Prohibited in F2

- Explaining why. That is F3, and it needs a decomposition with zero residual.
- Forecasting, scenarios, or "if this continues". That is F5.
- Cohort or funnel analysis. That is F4.
- Causal verbs: menyebabkan, akibat, dipicu, karena.
- A trend claim on fewer than six periods.
- Comparing a partial period against a full one without saying so.
- A mean quoted alone on a skewed measure.
- A correlation called a relationship in the causal sense.
- More than three charts.

## Exit check

All of these must be true before `advance_phase`:

- primary measure chosen and stated, with the rule that chose it
- headline produced in one of the two forms, with its base and group sizes
- period grain chosen, periods counted, partial periods identified and handled
- at least one contrast: a period change, or a group comparison when a category
  exists, with a `lainnya` bucket if needed and counts summing to the total
- distribution printed with mean and median side by side
- correlation printed with `n`, or the reason it was skipped
- concentration printed as two numbers, or the reason it was skipped
- at most three charts, each with its printed table
- `evidence/F2.json` written with one entry per claim
- `cannot_answer` updated for any dimension that was missing or unusable
