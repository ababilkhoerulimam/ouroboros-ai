# F2: What Happened

Question: **what changed, and in which segment?**

F2 measures. It does not explain. The moment you write "because", you have left this
phase.

## Entry condition

F1 is closed, and the metric eligibility table in `evidence/F1.json` names at least
one metric as computable. Pick one primary metric now and keep it for the whole
phase:

```
primary=revenue      # or orders, or units, depending on what F1 allowed
```

If F1 allowed revenue, use revenue. If not, use the next one down: orders, then
units. State the choice once in `notes/`, not as a finding. A metric choice is a
method, not a measurement.

## Step order

| # | Step | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | choose period grain and check coverage | one line: periods, rows per period, partial periods |
| 2 | trend of the primary metric | table of period, value, change vs baseline |
| 3 | segment decomposition | per segment: value, share, change, contribution to total change |
| 4 | concentration | top 20 percent share, items needed for 80 percent |
| 5 | direction check | does the aggregate direction hold inside the largest segments |

Steps 2 and 3 usually deserve a chart. Step 4 rarely does. Step 5 never does.

## Period selection

Pick the coarsest grain that still gives you enough points.

| Data span | Grain | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 18 months or more | month, plus the same month last year as baseline | seasonality visible |
| 6 to 17 months | month | no year-over-year claim |
| 8 weeks to 5 months | week, with day-of-week noted | weekly noise is large |
| under 8 weeks | no trend claim at all | report totals and levels only |

Fewer than six periods is a level comparison, not a trend. Do not draw a line
through four points and call it a trend; draw bars and call it a comparison.

Print the number of periods and the row count per period before anything else. A
period with suspiciously few rows is either a partial period or a data gap, and both
change the answer.

## Partial periods

The last period is almost always incomplete. The data was exported mid-month, so the
final month looks like a collapse.

- Compute days covered per period. Print them.
- If the final period is partial, do one of two things: exclude it and say so, or
  normalise every period to a daily rate and label the chart "per hari".
- Never compare a partial period against a full one without saying it in the chart
  and in the evidence statement.

This single mistake produces more false "penjualan turun" conclusions than any other
step in the run.

## Baseline

A change with no base is not a finding.

| Situation | Baseline |
|---|---|
| 13 months or more of data | the same period last year |
| 6 to 12 months | the previous period |
| fewer than 6 periods | the average of all periods, stated as such |
| a known event (promo, price change) | the period before the event, named |

Print the base next to the value: `revenue=362.4m base=412.0m base_period=2025-Q2
change=-12.4%`. The statement in evidence repeats the base in words.

## Segment decomposition

For each categorical column F1 marked usable with 2 to 12 distinct values, compute:

- value per segment for the latest period
- share of total
- change against the same baseline as the headline metric
- contribution to the total change, in absolute currency or count

Sort descending by contribution, not by value. The segment that moved the total is
not always the largest segment.

Above 12 distinct values, keep the top 10 by value and aggregate the rest into
`lainnya`. The bucket must be labelled, and the shares must still sum to 100 percent.
Print the residual after the bucket is added; if it is not zero, the grouping is
wrong.

Do not decompose by a column whose values changed meaning mid-period (a renamed
product, a merged region). If F1 flagged that, skip the column and record why.

## Concentration

Two numbers, both printed:

1. Share of the primary metric held by the top 20 percent of items (by count of
   items, not by value).
2. How many items are needed to reach 80 percent of the metric.

Then one sentence in evidence: concentrated or not, with the threshold you used.
Concentration above 60 percent in the top quintile is a finding worth reporting,
because it changes what an action should target: a few items, not the catalogue.

Do not compute Gini or HHI here. They are precise and the owner cannot act on them.

## Direction check

Before you claim a direction, look inside the two or three largest segments.

If the aggregate fell but the largest segments rose, the fall came from a small
segment or from mix. Do not state the aggregate direction as the finding. Record it
as an anomaly and let F3 explain it.

This is the cheapest version of the Simpson's Paradox audit. F3 does the full one.

## Chart budget

At most three charts in F2. The report has room for three in this section, and a
chart you draw but do not cite is deleted.

| Chart | Worth drawing when |
|---|---|
| trend line of the primary metric | six or more periods |
| sorted bar of segments by contribution | one dominant dimension exists |
| cumulative concentration curve | concentration is the finding |

Every chart follows `style.md`: title states the finding, bars from zero, direct
labels, and the same cell prints the table.

## Evidence discipline

Evidence is per claim, not per row. A table of twelve products yields one to three
findings, not twelve.

- The headline change: one `E-###`.
- The segment that drove it: one `E-###`.
- The concentration number: one `E-###`, or two if both numbers will be quoted.
- A segment with a small absolute contribution is not evidence, even if its
  percentage change is large. Percentages on small bases are noise.

Every statement carries the base: "pendapatan turun 12,4% pada Q3 2025 dibanding
Q2 2025", never "pendapatan turun".

## Prohibited in F2

- Explaining why. That is F3, and it needs a decomposition with zero residual.
- Forecasting, scenarios, or "if this continues". That is F5.
- Cohort or funnel analysis. That is F4.
- Causal verbs: menyebabkan, akibat, dipicu, karena.
- A trend claim on fewer than six periods.
- Comparing a partial period against a full one without saying so.
- More than three charts.

## Exit check

All of these must be true before `advance_phase`:

- primary metric chosen and stated, with the F1 verdict that allowed it
- period grain chosen, periods counted, partial periods identified and handled
- headline change printed with its base
- at least one segment decomposition, with a `lainnya` bucket if needed and shares
  summing to 100 percent
- concentration printed as two numbers
- direction check done, and any reversal recorded as an anomaly
- at most three charts, each with its printed table
- `evidence/F2.json` written with one entry per claim
- `cannot_answer` updated for any dimension that was missing or unusable
