# F4: Who & When

Question: **who stays, and at which step do they leave?**

F2 measured how much changed. F3 split that change into components. F4 answers a
different question: is the change coming from the same people behaving differently,
or from a different mix of people arriving and leaving? Those two produce different
actions, and only one of them is visible in a total.

F4 has two instruments, and both are optional. Retention needs a person. Funnel
needs a stage. If the data has neither, F4 does not run, and saying so is the
correct output.

## Entry condition

F1's metric table decides whether F4 has work. Check two verdicts:

```
customer_retention = computable   # a customer identifier exists
stage_progression  = computable   # an ordered stage or status column exists
```

| Verdict | F4 does |
|---|---|
| retention computable | cohort retention |
| funnel computable | stage funnel and drop-off |
| both | retention first, funnel second, one chart total |
| neither | skip the phase, see below |

## When F4 does not apply

Do not invent a customer. An invoice number is not a customer: every invoice is
unique by construction, so treating it as a person yields a cohort that never
returns, and a retention chart that says 0 percent forever.

Before skipping, print the evidence for the skip:

```
id_candidates=invoice_id,customer_id  repeat_rate_invoice_id=0.0000  repeat_rate_customer_id=0.4180
```

A key is a customer key only when it repeats across periods. Print the share of rows
whose key appears in more than one period. If no candidate key repeats, there is no
retention to measure.

Then close the phase properly:

1. Write `cannot_answer`: "retensi pelanggan: tidak ada identifier pelanggan" and
   "funnel: tidak ada kolom tahapan".
2. Write `evidence/F4.json` with the skip finding: which columns were tested and
   their repeat rates. A skip is a measurement of the data, and it carries an
   `E-###` like any other.
3. `advance_phase("F5", "F4 tidak berlaku: <reason>")`. The controller records it in
   `done.json` under `phases_skipped`.

Do not draw an empty chart to fill the section. Do not write a paragraph explaining
that retention would have been interesting.

## Is the key usable

A repeat rate above zero is necessary, not sufficient.

- Normalise before counting: trim whitespace, lower-case emails, strip phone
  prefixes and separators, collapse obvious typos in the same name. Print distinct
  counts before and after normalisation; a merge that halves the distinct count is a
  finding, not a cleanup detail.
- Print coverage: the share of rows with a non-null key. Guests and anonymous
  checkouts are usually null, and those customers are invisible, not churned.
- Coverage below 70 percent makes retention a statement about identified customers
  only. Say that in the evidence statement and set `confidence: "medium"`.
- One person with two identifiers (phone on one order, email on another) is two
  customers to a naive groupby. If both columns exist, print how often they disagree
  on the same order before trusting either.

## Step order

| # | Step | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | key test: repeat rate per candidate, coverage, normalisation effect | `key=… repeat=… coverage=…` |
| 2 | cohort definition: first purchase period per customer | cohort sizes per period |
| 3 | retention matrix: cohort × periods since acquisition | counts and rates, future cells masked |
| 4 | fixed-horizon retention: M1, M3, M6 across cohorts | one rate per horizon, with cohort count |
| 5 | new versus returning split of the primary metric | revenue and order share |
| 6 | stage order test, then funnel counts | counts per stage, step and cumulative rates |
| 7 | drop-off per step, with the worst step named | count lost, rate, share of total loss |
| 8 | segment contrast, one dimension only | retention or conversion by segment, with n |

## Cohort retention

A cohort is the set of customers whose first purchase falls in one period. Retention
at M*k* is the share of that cohort with at least one purchase in the k-th period
after acquisition.

```
retention_M1 = customers_active_in_M1 / cohort_size
```

Rules that change the answer:

- **Cohort size is the denominator, always printed.** A retention rate without its
  cohort size cannot be judged: 50 percent of 4 customers is noise.
- **Survivorship: keep everyone in the denominator.** Never filter the cohort to
  customers still active at the end of the window, to those who never returned an
  item, or to those who answered a survey. A cohort defined after the outcome
  measures the survivors, not the cohort. If the export only supports the filtered
  view, say so, set `confidence: "medium"`, and name the excluded group.
- **Mask the future, never zero it.** A cohort acquired last month has no M3. Leave
  the cell empty and label the matrix axis "belum teramati". A zero and an
  unobserved period look identical in a heatmap and mean opposite things.
- **Compare cohorts at a common horizon.** Averaging every available cell across
  cohorts of different ages weights young cohorts toward M1 and makes retention
  appear to improve. Report M1, M3, and M6 separately, each over the cohorts old
  enough to have that horizon.
- **Censoring at the window edge.** If the export ends mid-month, the final period
  is partial for everyone. Either drop it or normalise to a daily rate, exactly as
  F2 does, and say which.
- **Retention is not repeat rate.** Repeat rate is the share of customers with two
  or more orders in the whole window; retention is activity in a specific period. A
  customer can be retained at M1 and never return, so the two numbers differ and
  must not be quoted interchangeably.
- **Short windows.** With fewer than three complete months after acquisition, M1 is
  the only horizon that exists. Report M1 and write the rest into `cannot_answer`
  rather than extrapolating.

## Funnel

A funnel needs an order, not just stages. Print the distinct stage values and their
counts before anything else; a typo or a synonym in that column splits a stage in
two and lowers every downstream rate.

Establish the order from the data. If the column is a category with no progression
(product type, city), there is no funnel, and that goes in `cannot_answer`. If the
order is an assumption, state it once and mark every rate built on it as an
assumption, not a measurement.

Terminal states are exits, not steps. Cancelled, refunded, and expired orders leave
the funnel before step one; count them separately and report them as their own line.
A funnel that ends in "cancelled" measures nothing.

State which counting model you used, because they are not interchangeable:

| Model | Data shape | Counts | What it can answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| snapshot | one row per order, current status | exclusive: each order sits at its furthest stage | where orders are now |
| event log | one row per transition | cumulative: reached-set per stage | where orders stopped |

A snapshot cannot show earlier progress that was overwritten by a later status, and
an event log cannot be read as if each stage held only its own count. Mixing the two
double counts.

Print both rates for every step:

```
step_rate_i = reached_i / reached_{i-1}
cum_rate_i  = reached_i / reached_1
```

Reporting only the final conversion hides the step that actually costs orders.

## Drop-off

- Report the loss per step in absolute count, not only as a percentage. A 90 percent
  loss from a tiny stage and a 20 percent loss from the biggest stage are different
  problems, and only the counts tell them apart.
- Name the worst step by absolute loss and, separately, the worst step by rate. They
  are often different steps. State both, and say which one you are acting on.
- `share_of_total_loss = lost_i / (reached_1 - reached_last)` must sum to 1.0 across
  steps. Print the sum; if it is not 1.0, a stage is counted twice or missing.
- Below 30 orders at a step, the rate is noise. Print n, set `confidence: "medium"`,
  and do not rank that step as the worst.
- Fix one time window for every order in the funnel, counted from order creation.
  Without it, older orders had more time to convert and the funnel measures age
  instead of friction.
- Money at risk: convert lost orders to rupiah only when revenue per converted order
  is computable. Then print both inputs and the product. If it is not computable,
  report counts only and put the currency version in `cannot_answer`.

## Segment contrast

One dimension, never two. Two dimensions multiply the cells and most of them end up
under 30 observations.

- Retention: M1 rate per segment, with the cohort size of each segment.
- Funnel: conversion per segment, with the reached count at each end.
- Print the largest and the smallest segment side by side. A table of ten segments
  is a data dump, not a finding.
- Direction check: if the aggregate rate is flat while every large segment improves,
  the mix shifted. This is the same Simpson test as F3, applied to rates. Record it
  as an anomaly and do not present the aggregate as the finding.

## Writing the explanation

Three sentences, in this order:

1. The number: "Dari 412 pesanan, 268 sampai ke pengiriman (65,0 persen) dan 198
   diterima (48,0 persen dari awal)."
2. The worst step: "Kehilangan terbesar ada di langkah pembayaran: 96 pesanan, atau
   61 persen dari seluruh kehilangan."
3. The limit: "Data tidak mencatat alasan pembatalan, jadi penyebab putus di langkah
   ini tidak dapat dipastikan."

If the phase was skipped, the report body says nothing about retention or funnel. The
skip becomes one bullet in "Yang tidak bisa dijawab", and the evidence file keeps the
repeat rates that justified it.

## Chart budget

At most one chart in F4. The report has room for one in this section, and the two
instruments compete for it.

| Situation | Chart |
|---|---|
| retention with at least 3 cohorts and M1 to M3 observed | cohort heatmap, percentage printed in each cell, unobserved cells blank |
| funnel, no usable cohorts | horizontal funnel bars, counts at every step, step rate annotated |
| both available | draw the one whose finding is stronger; the other becomes a table |

The heatmap must not use a rainbow colormap, must print the percentage inside each
cell, and must leave unobserved cells empty rather than pale. The funnel bars share
one origin and print counts, because a bar of percentages cannot be checked against
the order table.

## Evidence discipline

Evidence is per claim, not per row.

| Claim | Entries |
|---|---|
| the key repeats, and how much of it is covered | one `E-###` each |
| cohort size for every cohort the report quotes | one `E-###` each |
| each retention horizon quoted | one `E-###`, naming the horizon, the cohorts, and the denominator |
| new versus returning share of revenue | one `E-###` |
| funnel counts per stage | one `E-###` |
| the worst step's loss | one `E-###`, count and rate |
| "retensi terlihat stabil" | none. That is a sentence, not a measurement |

A rate whose base is not in the same statement is not evidence, exactly as in F2.

## Prohibited in F4

- Treating an invoice, order, or session id as a customer.
- A cohort whose denominator is not printed.
- Zero-filling unobserved future periods.
- Averaging retention across cohorts of different maturity.
- Calling a missing identifier churn.
- A funnel with an invented stage order, or a stage order that is not printed.
- Mixing snapshot and event-log counting in one funnel.
- Ranking a step with n below 30 as the worst step.
- Converting lost orders to rupiah without a computable average order value.
- Predicting retention, lifetime value, or survival. That is modelling, and F5 bans
  it as well.
- More than one chart.
- A paragraph about a phase that was skipped.

## Exit check

All of these must be true before `advance_phase`:

- applicability decided and printed, with the repeat rate and coverage behind it
- if skipped: `cannot_answer` written, skip finding in `evidence/F4.json`, and the
  reason passed to `advance_phase`
- cohort size printed for every retention number reported
- unobserved periods masked, not zeroed
- each horizon reported separately, over cohorts old enough to have it
- funnel counting model stated, stage order printed, terminal states pulled out
- step and cumulative rates printed with counts
- worst step named by absolute loss and by rate, with the two distinguished
- loss shares summing to 1.0
- direction check done on the largest segments, with any reversal recorded
- at most one chart, with its printed table
- `evidence/F4.json` written with one entry per claim
- `cannot_answer` updated for every horizon or stage that could not be measured
